# Prompt 14 — restricted attested repair model (provisional, NOT a novel theorem)
## Definitions
Let G=(V,E) be a publicly known labelled finite graph and F subseteq E a hidden set of failed edges with |F|<=k. Theta is a finite explicitly encoded family of such failure sets, and the physical graph is G-F. S is a finite family of allowed end-to-end probes. Each probe s returns a binary success/failure value determined by whether its designated route survives F; optionally a rational noisy channel Q_s(y|F) is specified. An authenticated observation is a signed record (s,y,nonce,provenance), verified by a fixed public algorithm A. Authentication attests to origin and record integrity, **not** to the truth of a faulty sensor; any claimed sensor accuracy must be an explicit physical assumption.
A policy chooses probes and graph edits adaptively within horizon H, each at a positive rational cost. Permitted edits T are explicitly enumerated insertion/replacement/repair of edges, with a declared model of whether repairs reveal failures. The terminal predicate L(F,tau) is s-t connectivity in the physically repaired graph. The public checker V receives the instance, signed records, edit log and witness path; it validates provenance and logical consistency of the *declared* failure set, but cannot directly verify unmeasured physical reality.
A robust policy must satisfy L(F,tau(h)) for **all** F consistent with an admissible authenticated transcript h. A chance policy satisfies Pr[L(F,tau(h))] >=1-delta under a fully specified prior and sensor kernel. Cost is total probe+attestation+repair+witness-generation+verification work; expected and worst-case cost are distinct objectives. Nonadaptive comparator commits to a probe schedule before observations but may select final edits and witnesses after seeing them; the same verifier, horizon and edits apply.

## Baseline reductions and collisions
- If the graph edits are disabled, this reduces to fault localization/identifiability under end-to-end path measurements (E059–E062).
- If authenticated records are modeled as ordinary observations with an extra cost, the finite-horizon model is a POMDP (A11.1). This is a **semantic**, not necessarily polynomial-size, reduction.
- If uncertainty is polyhedral with suitable fixed-description assumptions, DDID models E050 may apply; do not claim a general compact MILP.
- If a single universal repair makes every F feasible, robust sensing may be unnecessary.
- If distinct failure sets have identical observation distributions and disjoint valid repair choices, robust zero-error repair is impossible (A11.3).
- Cryptographic signature verification does not transform a sensor reading into ground truth; a valid signature can authenticate a false sensor measurement.

## Restricted falsifiable question Q14
For **a natural specified family of sparse failed-edge graphs**, fixed end-to-end probing routes, positive attestation costs, and a fixed polynomial-time certificate checker, does the *minimax total cost of robust repair with authenticated path measurements* exhibit a tight structural lower/upper bound **not reducible** to existing network tomography identifiability, POMDP policy optimization, or priced stochastic probing bounds?
The question is provisional: neither a separating graph family nor an original bound has been established. **Do not freeze it as a proven novel problem.** Prompt 15 must first attempt explicit counterexamples and literature reductions. The model is frozen as a *test specification*, not as an originality claim.

## Assumption and quantifier hazards
A single path observation can fail due to multiple edges; absence of unique identification need not preclude successful universal repair. Worst-case sensing counts cannot be inferred from entropy without distribution and error constraints. Proof length depends on proof language. Robust guarantee is about physical failures, not merely encoded observations. If edits change routes, future probe semantics and schedule comparability must be recomputed.
