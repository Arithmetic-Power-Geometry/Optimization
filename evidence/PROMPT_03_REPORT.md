# Prompt 03 — canine olfaction, infotaxis and plume tracking
Date: 2026-10-20. Evidence IDs E011–E016 added; E001 (infotaxis) revisited, not duplicated.

## Inspection levels
E011,E013,E014: accessible primary full text inspected, with emphasis on abstracts, methods, and reported results; not an exhaustive replication. E012,E015,E016: primary publisher/PubMed abstract and metadata inspected. E016 complete author list remains pending; BibTeX intentionally flags this. No empirical reproduction.

## Evidence
- E011: Anatomically grounded fluid simulations and experimental sniffing data suggest each nostril can sample spatially separate odor signals. Does not prove a universal algorithmic advantage.
- E012: Active sniffing can improve odorant transport; mechanical biology is not an optimization theorem.
- E013: In cyborg moth experiments, reactive surge/casting with memory-like zigzagging can yield shorter paths than infotaxis, while infotaxis can be more robust to weak/no stimuli. IMPORTANT CONTRARY EVIDENCE to blanket superiority claims. The article also has a published correction (DOI 10.1371/journal.pcbi.1004019), to be audited in a later citation-quality gate.
- E014: Infotaxis with Bayesian posterior, intermittent observations and source-search reward was already implemented on robots. Thus belief updating from scent events is established.
- E015: Biological plume-tracking behaviors, spatial sensor arrays and active sampling were already transferred to robots in 2001.
- E016: Stimulus-dependent canine nostril preference is a biological observation, not a theorem on optimized structural creation.
- E001: Infotaxis already handles absent smooth gradients and sporadic cues; retained existing registry ID.

## Novelty consequence
NOT NEW: bilateral sensing, scent intermittency, active sniffing, reactive casting, memory-aided search, Bayesian olfactory source localization, olfactory robots.
UNVERIFIED: sensory acquisition used to decide certificate-valid structural transformations under explicit cost budgets. Standard POMDP/stochastic design formulations may already encode this; Prompt 04 must challenge the gap.

## Proposed formal mechanisms (hypotheses, not claims of novelty)
1. Binary or multichannel observations with spatially correlated likelihoods.
2. State-dependent detection/non-detection and cost-aware re-sensing.
3. Finite-memory fallback policy after information loss.
4. Transformation selection and certificate checking conditioned on posterior evidence.
Distinguish a probabilistic observation from a formal proof of structural validity.

## Limitations and future searches
This is targeted mining, not exhaustive systematic review. Several findings concern moths, not dogs; transfer to canine behavior cannot be assumed. The latest dog-inspired optimization heuristics, bilateral sensing under adversarial noise, odor memory, and robotic sensor fusion need additional citation-chaining. The biological results alone do not establish a new optimization paradigm.
